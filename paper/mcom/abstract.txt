Assuming the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, the explicit formula bounds the analytic rank of an elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{Q}$ in terms of the traces $a_p$, $p<e^{2L}$, for any admissible test function supported on $[-L,L]$. In rank computations this test function is usually fixed to a Fejér-type kernel. Optimising it for an individual $L$-function goes back to Booker; an earlier test on quadratic twists of rank about $7$ found little gain. We choose, for each curve, the test function that minimises the bound within a finite-dimensional space supported on $[-L,L]$. The minimiser solves a linear system with the Gram matrix of the Weil quadratic form of $E$, and the optimal bound is the reciprocal of the associated reproducing kernel at the central point, which we call the radix Riemanniana of $E$. Since the Fejér-type kernel lies in this space, the optimised bound is never weaker. Every bound is certified with ball arithmetic and checked against the zero side computed from the zeros of $L(E,s)$. On $1642$ curves from Cremona's tables and $2500$ from the Stein-Watkins database the number of primes needed to determine the analytic rank decreases by $8$-$23\%$ in aggregate. The gain grows sharply for large rank and large conductor: on all $395$ curves of rank $24$-$31$ in the ICARM leaderboard, with $p<10^{10}$, the optimised kernel certifies $r_{\mathrm{an}}\le r$ on $379$ curves and the fixed kernel on $372$, and where both succeed the fixed kernel needs a median of $4.0$ times as many primes. For the current record, the rank-$32$ curve #4239 of Elkies, Klagsbrun and Li, the optimised kernel certifies $r_{\mathrm{an}}\le 32$ under GRH with $2.53\cdot 10^{9}$ primes, the first upper bound for this curve, while the fixed kernel remains $0.39$ above the target. We validate the fixed-kernel baseline by reproducing all published values of Bober and of Klagsbrun, Sherman and Weigandt.
