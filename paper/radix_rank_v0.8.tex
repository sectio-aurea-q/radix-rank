\begin{abstract}
Assuming the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH), the explicit formula bounds the analytic rank of an elliptic curve $E/\Q$ by a quantity computed from the traces $a_p$, $p<e^{2L}$, for every admissible test function supported on $[-L,L]$. Usually it is a fixed Fej\'er-type kernel; we choose it for each curve. Within a finite-dimensional space the optimal bound is the reciprocal of a reproducing kernel at the central point, the \emph{radix Riemanniana} of $E$. Under GRH we prove that the best bound over all continuous test functions supported in $[-2L,2L]$ with non-negative Fourier transform is attained at a reproducing kernel of the Weil form, and that the finite-dimensional bounds converge to it. Every bound behind a rank statement is certified in ball or interval arithmetic. On $4142$ curves from the Cremona and Stein--Watkins databases the number of primes needed to certify $\ran\le r$, $r$ the recorded rank, decreases by $8$--$23\%$ in aggregate, and by much more at high rank and large conductor. On the $395$ curves of rank $24$--$31$ of the ICARM leaderboard (17 October 2026) the optimised kernel certifies $\ran\le r$ on $394$, the fixed kernel, given at least as many primes, on $372$. For the rank-$32$ record curve of Elkies, Klagsbrun and Li the optimised kernel gave the first upper bound matching the $32$ known independent points. Jain's $2$-Selmer bounds settle the rank of $394$ of these $395$ curves; ours settle the remaining one, \#1902, assuming in addition the Birch--Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let $E/\Q$ be an elliptic curve of conductor $N$ and let $\ran(E)$ be the order of vanishing of $L(E,s)$ at the central point. Under the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH), Mestre's explicit-formula method~\cite{Mestre} bounds $\ran(E)$ from above by a quantity computed from the traces of Frobenius $a_p$ for $p$ below a cutoff. Bober~\cite{Bober} made this practical with a Fej\'er-type test function, and Klagsbrun, Sherman and Weigandt~\cite{KSW} used the same function to bound the analytic rank of
the Elkies curve by $28$ under GRH. Their accompanying result that its Mordell--Weil rank equals $28$
rests on computing the $2$-ranks of class groups of associated cubic fields, not on the explicit
formula; the method discussed here bounds the analytic rank only. In~\cite{Bober,KSW} the shape of the test function is fixed, and only its support (the cutoff) is varied. Bober reports, however, that Booker and Dwyer bounded the analytic rank of the Elkies curve by $28$ under GRH by optimising the test function as in~\cite[\S3]{Booker} (\cite[Remark~1.2]{Bober}; see also~\cite[footnote~21]{Watkins}).

This paper asks a simple question: \emph{for a given curve and a given cutoff, which test function gives the best bound?} Within any finite-dimensional space of admissible functions supported on $[-L,L]$ the answer is explicit. The explicit formula expresses the bound as a ratio of a quadratic form (the Weil form of $E$, evaluated through the $a_p$) to the square of a linear functional. The minimiser is therefore obtained from one linear solve with a Gram matrix. The optimal value is the reciprocal of the reproducing kernel at $0$ of the space equipped with the Weil inner product.

\subsection*{What is known, and what is new}
The Weil quadratic form restricted to functions supported on a finite interval, and its Gram matrices with respect to a basis, have been studied for the Riemann zeta function by Yoshida~\cite{Yoshida}, by Connes, Consani and Moscovici~\cite{CCM}, by Suzuki~\cite{Suzuki} via screw functions and de Branges spaces, and recently by Shi~\cite{Shi} as finite ``Prime--Weil matrices''. For $\zeta$, Suzuki~\cite{SuzukiCJM} constructed under RH the Hilbert space obtained by completing $C_c^\infty(\R)$ with respect to the Weil form and identified it with a de Branges space, and in~\cite{Suzuki} he studies the restriction of the Weil form to $L^2(-a,a)$ as a closed symmetric form. Reproducing-kernel methods for Fourier optimisation problems in which the input is a statistic of zeros (pair correlation, or the one-level density of a family) rather than data of an individual $L$-function have been developed by Carneiro, Chandee, Littmann and Milinovich~\cite{CCLM} and by Carneiro, Chirre and Milinovich~\cite{CCMil}.

Optimising the test function for an \emph{individual} $L$-function is not new. Booker~\cite[\S3]{Booker} writes the explicit-formula sum over zeros as a positive definite quadratic form in the coefficients of a finite family of test functions and minimises it under a linear normalisation by inverting the matrix, in order to locate zeros of Artin $L$-functions. Watkins, Donnelly, Elkies, Fisher, Granville and Rogers~\cite[\S7]{Watkins} applied this extension to analytic-rank bounds for quadratic twists and found the improvement to be small, typically about $0.05$ (for example $7.138$ to $7.090$ with $10$ basis functions, $7.089$ with $40$), and therefore used the fixed kernel; they note that where the computation of the $a_p$ dominates the running time ``there would be no reason (other than simplicity of implementation) not to use it''~\cite[p.~75]{Watkins}. Booker~\cite[\S3, (3.3)]{Booker} also defines the infimum of the zero sum over all admissible test functions with given support, following Booker and Str\"ombergsson~\cite{BS} for the Selberg trace formula; under GRH our $B^*(L)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is this infimum for the support $[-2L,2L]$ and the point $0$. Proposition~\ref{prop:opt} below is the same minimisation in a Legendre basis on the window. We claim no novelty for this principle.

What this paper adds is (i) a proof that, under GRH, this infimum is attained, an identification of the extremal function as the reproducing kernel at $0$ of the Weil form, and the convergence of the finite-dimensional optima to it with an exact error identity (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}); (ii) certified output for every bound used for a rank statement; (iii) a systematic measurement of the gain against the fixed kernel on several thousand curves; and (iv) evidence that the gain, small for the quadratic twists of~\cite{Watkins} and enough on $E_{28}$ to give $\ran\le28$ in the computation of Booker and Dwyer reported in~\cite{Bober}, is large across the whole ICARM high-rank leaderboard, where these computations are most expensive. On that leaderboard this turns into certified upper bounds that match the known lower bounds, among them the first one for the current rank-$32$ record (Section~\ref{sec:records}), and into the bound $\ran\le r$ on $394$ of the $395$ curves of rank $24$--$31$ (Section~\ref{sec:board}). None of the certified bounds uses Theorems~\ref{thm:coercive} and~\ref{thm:main} or positive definiteness: each certificate evaluates the bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:bound} for one explicit test function.

\subsection*{Results in brief}
\begin{itemize}
\item The optimised bound is never weaker than the fixed-kernel bound at the same cutoff (Proposition~\ref{prop:dominate}). This is a consequence of the construction, not an empirical finding. The empirical question is \emph{how much} it gains.
\item Under GRH the optimised bounds converge, as the dimension $K$ grows, to $B^*(L)=1/\Rad(0,0)$, the reciprocal of the reproducing kernel of the Hilbert space defined by the Weil form; no continuous test function $g$ with $\supp g\subset[-2L,2L]$ and $\hat g\ge0$ gives a smaller bound (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}).
\item Every bound used for a rank statement is certified rigorously (values marked as floating point are not): in Arb ball arithmetic, or, for the board curves carried to $p<10^{10}$, the closing runs and the $2$-torsion curves, with the prime sum in outward-rounded interval arithmetic and the remaining terms in Arb. In
the benchmark files of Sections~\ref{sec:bench} and~\ref{sec:valid} the two endpoints of each
enclosure coincide when printed as doubles, because at those prime counts the radius falls below
double precision; the certified decision is the comparison of the upper endpoint with the target. No certified bound in our tests contradicts the rank recorded for the curve.
\item On four curves of small conductor the prime-side value of the Weil form agrees with the zero-side value computed from the zeros of $L(E,s)$. The discrepancy halves with each doubling of the height up to which zeros are included (Section~\ref{sec:zeros}).
\item The number of primes needed to certify $\ran\le r$ for the recorded rank $r$ (Section~\ref{sec:bench}) decreases in aggregate by $8.3$--$15.2\%$ (Cremona, $N<4.1\cdot 10^5$) and by $17.0$--$22.7\%$ (Stein--Watkins, $4.4\cdot10^6\le N\le 7.5\cdot 10^9$), depending on the stopping criterion. For individual curves of conductor up to $3.2\cdot 10^{16}$ the decrease ranges from $0\%$ to $43\%$.
\item On curves of record rank (Section~\ref{sec:records}) the gain is much larger. For the smallest-conductor curves of ranks $28$--$31$ in the ICARM leaderboard, the number of primes needed for a bound below $r+2$ drops by a factor of about $6.5$. For the bound below $r+1$ the fixed kernel fails for three of the four curves with all primes below $10^9$, while the optimised kernel succeeds with between $2.3\cdot10^5$ and $1.3\cdot10^7$ primes. On the historic record curves $E_{20}$--$E_{27}$ the factor is $2$--$3.5$.
\item For the current record curve \#4239 (rank $\ge32$) the optimised kernel certifies $\ran\le32$ under GRH with all $2\,529\,822\,599$ primes $p<e^{24.82}\approx6.014\cdot10^{10}$ --- and $\ran=32$ under GRH and BSD, using the $32$ independent points of the leaderboard. The fixed kernel does not reach the target: its certified bound at $L=12.40$, with
$2\,481\,819\,193$ primes, is $[34.39360281\pm4.7\cdot10^{-9}]$ (Section~\ref{sec:4239}).
\item On the $395$ leaderboard curves of rank $24$--$31$ (snapshot of 17 October 2026) the optimised kernel certifies $\ran\le r$ under GRH on $394$ curves: on $392$ with primes below $1.2\cdot10^{11}$, on \#3593 with primes below $2.8\cdot10^{11}$ and on \#535 with primes below $8\cdot10^{11}$. The fixed kernel certifies $372$ with $p<10^{10}$ and none of the other $23$ at any prime bound at which we ran it. Jain's $2$-Selmer bounds~\cite{Jain} determine the rank of $394$ of these curves, under GRH for finitely many Dedekind zeta functions; together with ours, under GRH for $L(E,s)$ and BSD, all $395$ are determined, \#1902 only by the explicit formula (Section~\ref{sec:board}).
\item On the $44$ leaderboard curves of rank $20$--$22$ with a rational point of order $2$, to which the class-group method of \texttt{rankbound} does not apply, the optimised kernel certifies $\ran\le r$ on all of them with at most $731\,001$ primes, while the fixed kernel needs a median of $2.35$ times as many primes (Section~\ref{sec:torsion}).
\item The fixed-kernel baseline is exactly Bober's method: an independent implementation of Bober's formula reproduces all seven published values in~\cite{Bober,KSW}; it agrees with our floating-point evaluation, and on three curves with our Arb certificate, to the six decimals to which we tabulate it (Section~\ref{sec:valid}).
\end{itemize}

\section{The explicit formula and rank bounds}

Write $L(E,s)=\sum_{n\ge1} a_n n^{-s-1/2}$ so that the central point is $s=1/2$, and put
\[
\Lambda(E,s) = N^{s/2}(2\pi)^{-s}\,\Gamma(s+\tfrac12)\,L(E,s), \qquad \Lambda(E,s)=\pm\Lambda(E,1-s).
\]
For $p\nmid N$ let $\alpha_p,\beta_p$ be defined by $\alpha_p+\beta_p=a_p/\sqrt p$, $\alpha_p\beta_p=1$, and set $c_{p^k}=\alpha_p^k+\beta_p^k$. For $p\mid N$ set $c_{p^k}=(a_p/\sqrt p)^k$ (so $c_{p^k}=0$ for additive reduction). Then $-\frac{L'}{L}(E,s)=\sum_{n\ge2}\Lambda(n)c_n n^{-s}$ in the normalisation above.

Let $f\colon\R\to\R$ be even, bounded and supported in $[-L,L]$. Put $g=f*f$, so that $g$ is even and supported in $[-2L,2L]$, and
\[
\hat f(r)=\int_{\R} f(x)e^{irx}\,dx,\qquad h(r)=\hat g(r)=\hat f(r)^2 .
\]
Writing the nontrivial zeros as $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma$ (with multiplicity), the explicit formula reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit}
\sum_{\gamma} h(\gamma) \;=\; g(0)\log\frac{N}{4\pi^2}
\;+\;2\Big[-\gamma_{\mathrm E}\,g(0)+\int_0^{\infty}\frac{g(0)-g(u)}{e^{u}-1}\,du\Big]
\;-\;2\sum_{n\ge 2}\frac{\Lambda(n)\,c_n}{\sqrt n}\,g(\log n),
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_{\mathrm E}$ is Euler's constant. Only prime powers $n<e^{2L}$ contribute. We denote the right-hand side by $W_E(f)$. For smooth $f$ this is the classical explicit formula (see e.g.~\cite[Thm.~5.12]{IK}); under GRH, by Lemma~\ref{lem:formula}, it holds for every $f$ in the space $\Hs$ of Section~\ref{sec:optimal}, in particular for every polynomial on $[-L,L]$. For other bounded $f$ the right-hand side may be $+\infty$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bound}
Assume GRH for $L(E,s)$. Then for every $f$ as above with $\hat f(0)\ne0$,
\[
\ran(E)\;\le\;\frac{W_E(f)}{\hat f(0)^2}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:explicit} is $+\infty$ there is nothing to prove; otherwise $f\in\Hs$ and~\eqref{eq:explicit} holds by Lemma~\ref{lem:formula}. Under GRH every $\gamma$ is real. Since $f$ is real and even, $\hat f$ is real, so $h(\gamma)=\hat f(\gamma)^2\ge0$. The central zero contributes $\ran(E)\,\hat f(0)^2$ to the left-hand side of~\eqref{eq:explicit}, and every other term is nonnegative.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
For $f=\mathbf 1_{[-L,L]}$ one has $g(u)=(2L-|u|)_+$ and $h(r)=4\sin^2(Lr)/r^2$. Up to scaling, this is the Fej\'er-type test function used in~\cite{Bober,KSW}. We call the resulting bound $B_{\mathrm{std}}(L)$. With $L=\pi\Delta$ it coincides with Bober's $\sum_\gamma f(\gamma;\Delta)$: $\hat f(r)^2/\hat f(0)^2=(\sin(\Delta\pi r)/(\Delta\pi r))^2$, the conductor term becomes $\log(\sqrt N/2\pi)/(\pi\Delta)$, and the archimedean term evaluates to Spicer's closed form $-\gamma_{\mathrm E}/(\pi\Delta)+\big(\pi^2/6-\operatorname{Li}_2(e^{-2\pi\Delta})\big)/(2\pi^2\Delta^2)$ quoted in~\cite{KSW}.
\end{remark}

\section{The optimal window kernel}

The map $f\mapsto W_E(f)$ is a quadratic form, $W_E(f)=\mathcal B_E(f,f)$, where $\mathcal B_E$ is obtained from~\eqref{eq:explicit} by replacing $g=f*f$ with the symmetrised correlation of two functions. Fix the basis
\[
b_k(x)=P_{2k}(x/L)\,\mathbf 1_{[-L,L]}(x),\qquad 0\le k<K,
\]
of even polynomials on the window, where $P_m$ is the Legendre polynomial of degree $m$. Let $G_{ij}=\mathcal B_E(b_i,b_j)$ be the Gram matrix and $v_i=\hat b_i(0)=\int b_i$, so that $v=(2L,0,\dots,0)$. For $f=\sum c_k b_k$ we have $W_E(f)=c^{\mathsf T}Gc$ and $\hat f(0)=v^{\mathsf T}c$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:opt}
If $G$ is positive definite, the minimum of $W_E(f)/\hat f(0)^2$ over the $f$ in $\mathrm{span}(b_0,\dots,b_{K-1})$ with $\hat f(0)\ne0$ equals
\[
B_{\mathrm{rad}}(L)=\frac{1}{v^{\mathsf T}G^{-1}v},
\]
and is attained at $c^\ast=G^{-1}v/(v^{\mathsf T}G^{-1}v)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Minimise $c^{\mathsf T}Gc$ subject to $v^{\mathsf T}c=1$ (Lagrange multipliers, or Cauchy--Schwarz in the inner product $\langle x,y\rangle=x^{\mathsf T}Gy$).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Radix Riemanniana]\label{def:radix}
Let $\boldsymbol\beta(z)=(\hat b_0(z),\dots,\hat b_{K-1}(z))^{\mathsf T}$. For an elliptic curve $E/\Q$ and a window $[-L,L]$, the \emph{radix Riemanniana} of $E$ (of order $K$) is the kernel
\[
\mathcal R^{(K)}_{E,L}(z,w)\;=\;\boldsymbol\beta(w)^{\ast}\,G^{-1}\,\boldsymbol\beta(z),
\]
that is, the reproducing kernel of the space $\{\hat f: f\in\mathrm{span}(b_k)\}$ equipped with the Weil inner product $\mathcal B_E$, linear in $z$ and antilinear in $w$. It is built from the Gamma factor, the conductor and the primes $p<e^{2L}$ alone, without reference to the zeros of $L(E,s)$. Since $v=\boldsymbol\beta(0)$, the optimal bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:opt} is
\[
B_{\mathrm{rad}}(L)=\frac{1}{\mathcal R^{(K)}_{E,L}(0,0)}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(iv) and its proof, $\mathcal R^{(K)}_{E,L}(z,0)\to\mathcal R_{E,L}(z,0)$ locally uniformly as $K\to\infty$, where $\mathcal R_{E,L}$ is the reproducing kernel of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}; since the $b_k$ are even, for general $w$ the limit is the kernel $\tfrac12\bigl(\mathcal R_{E,L}(z,w)+\mathcal R_{E,L}(z,-w)\bigr)$ of the even subspace. The radix Riemanniana is thus the window analogue, for $L(E,s)$, of the reproducing kernels of the Hilbert spaces attached to the Weil form of $\zeta$ in~\cite{SuzukiCJM,Suzuki}.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:dominate}
For every $L$ with $G$ positive definite, $B_{\mathrm{rad}}(L)\le B_{\mathrm{std}}(L)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\mathbf 1_{[-L,L]}=b_0$ lies in the span, so $B_{\mathrm{std}}(L)$ is one of the values minimised over in Proposition~\ref{prop:opt}.
\end{proof}

Positive definiteness of $G$ holds under GRH: $W_E(f)=\sum_\gamma\hat f(\gamma)^2\ge0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:formula}, and it
vanishes only if $\hat f$ vanishes on the whole zero set; since $\hat f$ is entire of exponential type
$L$ while the number of zeros of $L(E,s)$ with $0<\gamma\le T$ is $\sim(T\log T)/\pi$ and, under GRH, $\gg T\log\log T$ of them are distinct, this forces $f=0$ (see Lemma~\ref{lem:formula} for the details, including multiplicities). In practice we simply attempt a Cholesky factorisation and discard the window if it fails. Note that \emph{the certificate in Section~\ref{sec:cert} does not depend on optimality or on positive definiteness}: it evaluates $W_E(f)$ for the specific $f$ that was found.

\section{The limiting radix kernel is optimal}\label{sec:optimal}

Proposition~\ref{prop:opt} optimises within a space of dimension $K$, and numerically the bound saturates already for $K\approx10$ (Section~\ref{sec:4239}). We now show, assuming GRH, that the bounds $B_K(L)$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:opt} with $K$ basis functions converge as $K\to\infty$ to the best bound obtainable from any continuous test function supported in $[-2L,2L]$ with non-negative Fourier transform, and we identify the limit and the extremal function. A first version of this section appeared as a separate note~\cite{HacOpt}.

In this section $f$ may be complex-valued; put $f^\star(x)=\overline{f(-x)}$ and $g=f*f^\star$, so that $\hat g=|\hat f|^2$ and $\supp g\subset[-2L,2L]$; for real even $f$ this is the function $g=f*f$ of Section~2. Let $w_n=\Lambda(n)c_n/\sqrt n$. For every continuous $g$ with $\supp g\subset[-2L,2L]$ and $\hat g\ge0$ put
\[
  \mathcal W_E(g)=g(0)\log\frac{N}{4\pi^2}+\mathrm{Arch}(g)-2\sum_{n<e^{2L}}w_n\operatorname{Re}g(\log n),
\]
\[
  \mathrm{Arch}(g)=2\Bigl[-\gamma_{\mathrm E}\,g(0)+\int_0^{2L}\frac{g(0)-\operatorname{Re}g(u)}{e^u-1}\,du-g(0)\log\bigl(1-e^{-2L}\bigr)\Bigr],
\]
with values in $(-\infty,+\infty]$. Since $g$ is continuous and $\hat g\ge0$, we have $\hat g\in L^1(\R)$ (Fej\'er summation at $0$), hence $g(u)=\frac1{2\pi}\int\hat g(t)e^{-itu}\,dt$ and $g(0)\ge|g(u)|$; so the integrand in $\mathrm{Arch}(g)$ is non-negative. Because $g$ vanishes on $(2L,\infty)$, for $g=f*f$ with $f$ as in Section~2 this is the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:explicit}, that is, $W_E(f)=\mathcal W_E(f*f)$, and for smooth $g$ the explicit formula gives $\mathcal W_E(g)=\sum_\gamma\hat g(\gamma)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:symbol}
For every $f\in L^2(\R)$ with support in $[-L,L]$,
\[
  \mathcal W_E(f*f^\star)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_\R|\hat f(t)|^2M(t)\,dt,\qquad
  M(t)=\log\frac{N}{4\pi^2}+2\operatorname{Re}\psi(1+it)-2\sum_{n<e^{2L}}w_n\cos(t\log n),
\]
where $\psi=\Gamma'/\Gamma$. $M$ is real and even, and $|M(t)-2\log(e+|t|)|\le C_{E,L}$ for all $t\in\R$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
From $\psi(z)=-\gamma_{\mathrm E}+\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-u}-e^{-zu}}{1-e^{-u}}\,du$ we get
$\operatorname{Re}\psi(1+it)=-\gamma_{\mathrm E}+\int_0^\infty\frac{1-\cos tu}{e^u-1}\,du$. Since
$g(u)=\frac1{2\pi}\int|\hat f|^2e^{-itu}\,dt$, we have $g(0)-\operatorname{Re}g(u)=\frac1{2\pi}\int|\hat f|^2(1-\cos tu)\,dt$, and
$g(u)=0$ for $u>2L$. Tonelli's theorem (the integrand is non-negative) gives
$\frac1{2\pi}\int|\hat f|^2\,2\operatorname{Re}\psi(1+it)\,dt=\mathrm{Arch}(g)$, including the case where both sides are $+\infty$.
The prime sum is finite. The bound follows from $\operatorname{Re}\psi(1+it)=\log|t|+O(t^{-2})$ for $|t|\ge1$.
\end{proof}

So $\mathcal W_E$ is a truncated Wiener--Hopf (continuous Toeplitz) form with symbol $M$ on the Paley--Wiener space. The symbol takes negative values, but under GRH the form is positive. Let $\mu(t)=2\log(e+|t|)$, let $\Hs$ be the space of $f\in L^2(\R)$ with support in $[-L,L]$ and
\[
  \|f\|_\mu^2=\frac1{2\pi}\int_\R|\hat f|^2\mu<\infty,
\]
and put $Q(f,h)=\frac1{2\pi}\int\hat f\,\overline{\hat h}\,M$, $Q(f)=Q(f,f)$, and, once $Q$ is known to be positive definite, $\|f\|_Q=Q(f)^{1/2}$.
$\Hs$ is a Hilbert space, $|Q(f)|\le C\|f\|_\mu^2$, and $\mathcal W_E(f*f^\star)=+\infty$ for $f\notin\Hs$. The indicator $\mathbf 1_{[-L,L]}$, which generates the Fej\'er-type kernel, and every polynomial restricted to $[-L,L]$ lie in $\Hs$; in particular so does the span of the $b_k$ of Section~3.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:compact}
The embedding $\Hs\hookrightarrow L^2(\R)$ is compact.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\|f\|_\mu\le1$. Since $\mu\ge2$, $\|f\|_2\le1/\sqrt2$, so the functions $\hat f$ are uniformly bounded, and uniformly Lipschitz because
$|\hat f'|\le\int|xf|\le L\sqrt L$. A subsequence therefore converges locally uniformly (Arzel\`a--Ascoli), while
$\frac1{2\pi}\int_{|t|>T}|\hat f|^2\le1/(2\log(e+T))$ uniformly. By Plancherel the subsequence is Cauchy in $L^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:formula}
Assume GRH for $L(E,s)$. For $f\in\Hs$, $\sum_\gamma|\hat f(\gamma)|^2=Q(f)$, the zeros counted with multiplicity; in particular $\ran\,|\hat f(0)|^2\le Q(f)$.
If $Q(f)=0$, then $f=0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For smooth $f$ this is the explicit formula (with equality) and Lemma~\ref{lem:symbol}. For general $f\in\Hs$, dilate
($f(x/(1-\delta))$) and mollify. The approximants $f_j$ are smooth with support in $[-L,L]$ and converge to $f$ in $\Hs$: on the Fourier side the
dilations are uniformly bounded on $L^2(\R,\mu\,dt)$ since $\mu(s/(1-\delta))\le C\mu(s)$, and they converge strongly on
$C_c(\R)$, which is dense there. Hence $Q(f_j)\to Q(f)$, and $\hat f_j\to\hat f$ uniformly on $\R$. Applying the explicit formula to the smooth differences $f_j-f_k$ gives $\sum_\gamma|\hat f_j(\gamma)-\hat f_k(\gamma)|^2=Q(f_j-f_k)\to0$, so $(\hat f_j(\gamma))_\gamma$ converges in $\ell^2$ (zeros with multiplicity), necessarily to $(\hat f(\gamma))_\gamma$, and $\sum_\gamma|\hat f(\gamma)|^2=\lim_jQ(f_j)=Q(f)$.

If $Q(f)=0$, then $\hat f(\gamma)=0$ for every ordinate $\gamma$. Under GRH the multiplicity $m(\gamma)$ of a zero at $\tfrac12+i\gamma$ satisfies $m(\gamma)\ll\log(N(|\gamma|+3))/\log\log(N(|\gamma|+3))$. Indeed, in the Riemann--von Mangoldt formula for $L(E,s)$ every term other than $S(t)=\frac1\pi\arg L(E,\tfrac12+it)$ is continuous in $t$, so $m(\gamma)$ is at most the jump of $S$ at $t=\gamma$, hence at most $2\sup_{|t-\gamma|\le1}|S(t)|$; and by \cite[Thm.~5, (4.4)]{CChM}, applied to $L(E,s)$ itself (self-dual, of degree $2$, with $\vartheta=0$ by Hasse's bound and spectral parameters $\tfrac12,\tfrac32$), $|S(t)|\ll\log(N(|t|+3))/\log\log(N(|t|+3))$. Since the number of
ordinates in $[-T,T]$ counted with multiplicity is $\frac T\pi\log\frac{NT^2}{(2\pi e)^2}+O(\log NT)$, there are
$\gg T\log\log T$ distinct ones. An entire function of exponential type has $O(R)$ zeros in $|z|\le R$ (Jensen's formula), so
$\hat f\equiv0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[coercivity]\label{thm:coercive}
Assume GRH for $L(E,s)$. There is $\lambda_{E,L}>0$ with $Q(f)\ge\lambda_{E,L}\|f\|_\mu^2$ for all $f\in\Hs$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $M=\mu+R$ with $R$ bounded. Then $Q(f)=\langle(I+T)f,f\rangle_\mu$, where $\langle Tf,h\rangle_\mu=\frac1{2\pi}\int\hat f\,
\overline{\hat h}\,R$. The operator $T$ factors through the compact embedding of Lemma~\ref{lem:compact}, so it is compact and
self-adjoint, and $\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(I+T)=\{1\}$. By the Riesz--Schauder theorem \cite[Thm.~VI.15]{RS}, $\sigma(I+T)\cap(-\infty,\tfrac12)$ consists of finitely many
eigenvalues, each positive by Lemma~\ref{lem:formula}. Hence $\min\sigma(I+T)>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Assume GRH for $L(E,s)$ and let $L>0$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item There is a unique $k=k_{E,L}\in\Hs$ with $Q(f,k)=\hat f(0)$ for all $f\in\Hs$; it is real and even. The space
      $\{\hat f:f\in\Hs\}$ with norm $\|\hat f\|=\|f\|_Q$ has a reproducing kernel $\Rad(z,w)$, linear in $z$, with
      $\hat f(w)=Q(f,k_w)$ where $\hat k_w=\Rad(\cdot,w)$; we have $\hat k(z)=\Rad(z,0)$ and $\Rad(0,0)=\hat k(0)=Q(k)>0$.
\item $\displaystyle B^*(L):=\inf_{0\ne f\in\Hs}\frac{Q(f)}{|\hat f(0)|^2}=\frac1{\Rad(0,0)}$, attained exactly by the
      multiples of $k$; in particular $\ran\le\lfloor B^*(L)\rfloor$.
\item For every continuous $g$ with $\supp g\subset[-2L,2L]$, $\hat g\ge0$ on $\R$ and $\hat g(0)>0$,
      \[
        \frac{\mathcal W_E(g)}{\hat g(0)}\ \ge\ B^*(L).
      \]
      Thus among all explicit-formula bounds whose test function lies in this class, so that only the coefficients
      $c_n$ with $n<e^{2L}$ enter, $B^*(L)$ is the smallest.
\item The bounds $B_K(L)=1/(v^{\mathsf T}G^{-1}v)$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:opt} (that is, $B_{\mathrm{rad}}(L)$ with $K$ basis functions) are non-increasing in $K$, $B_K(L)\to B^*(L)$, and
      \[
        B_K(L)-B^*(L)=\frac{\|k-P_Kk\|_Q^2}{\Rad(0,0)\,\|P_Kk\|_Q^2},
      \]
      where $P_K$ is the $Q$-orthogonal projection onto $V_K=\operatorname{span}\{b_0,\dots,b_{K-1}\}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) By Theorem~\ref{thm:coercive}, $(\Hs,Q)$ is a Hilbert space, and $\ell(f)=\hat f(0)=\langle f,\mathbf 1_{[-L,L]}\rangle$
is continuous, with $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ the inner product of $L^2(\R)$ (indeed $|\ell(f)|\le\sqrt L\|f\|_\mu$). The Riesz representation theorem gives $k$. More generally every point evaluation is continuous, $|\hat f(z)|\le\sqrt{2L}\,e^{L|\operatorname{Im}z|}\|f\|_2\le\sqrt{L/\lambda_{E,L}}\,e^{L|\operatorname{Im}z|}\|f\|_Q$, which gives the reproducing kernel. Since $M$ is real and even, $k(-x)$ and
$\overline{k}$ represent $\ell$ as well, so $k$ is real and even. Moreover $Q(k)=\ell(k)$, and $Q(k)>0$ as $\ell\ne0$.

(ii) Cauchy--Schwarz in $(\Hs,Q)$: $|\hat f(0)|^2=|Q(f,k)|^2\le Q(f)Q(k)$, with equality if and only if $f\in\C k$. The bound on
$\ran$ is Lemma~\ref{lem:formula}.

(iii) As noted after the definition of $\mathcal W_E$, $\hat g\in L^1(\R)$. Moreover $\hat g$ is entire of exponential type $\le2L$,
and real, bounded and non-negative on $\R$. Hence $\int\log^+\hat g(t)\,(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt<\infty$, and by Akhiezer's theorem~\cite{Akhiezer} (see~\cite[pp.~124--132]{Boas}), $\hat g=|F|^2$ on $\R$ with $F$ entire of exponential
type $\le L$. Then $F\in L^2(\R)$, so $F=\hat f$ with $\supp f\subset[-L,L]$ (Paley--Wiener), and $g=f*f^\star$. If
$f\notin\Hs$, then $\mathcal W_E(g)=+\infty$. Otherwise $\mathcal W_E(g)=Q(f)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:symbol}, $\hat g(0)=|\hat f(0)|^2$, and (ii)
applies.

(iv) $G$ is the Gram matrix of $Q$ on $V_K$, invertible since $Q$ is positive definite, and $v_j=\ell(b_j)$, so $v=(2L,0,\dots,0)$ by orthogonality of the Legendre polynomials. On $V_K$ the functional $\ell$ is represented by $P_Kk$, so $B_K=1/\|P_Kk\|_Q^2$ as in (ii); Pythagoras gives
the identity, and $B_K$ is non-increasing because the $V_K$ are nested. Even polynomials are dense in the even part of $\Hs$ (smooth even functions supported in $(-L,L)$ are dense there, by dilation and mollification with an even mollifier as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:formula}): for $h\in C^1[-L,L]$, integration by parts gives $|\widehat{h\mathbf 1_{[-L,L]}}(t)|\ll\|h\|_{C^1}/|t|$, hence
$\|h\mathbf 1_{[-L,L]}\|_\mu\ll\|h\|_{C^1}$, and smooth even functions supported in $(-L,L)$ are $C^1$-limits of
even polynomials. Since $k$ is even, the norm equivalence of Theorem~\ref{thm:coercive} gives $\|k-P_Kk\|_Q\to0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Part (iii) concerns certificates that use $\hat g\ge0$ on all of $\R$, which is what one needs when the zeros are unknown.
Certificates using further information (for example the parity or integrality of $\ran$, or signs of individual prime
terms) are not covered. On the curve \#4239, increasing $K$ from $10$ to $20$ changes the floating-point bound by $5\cdot10^{-5}$ (Section~\ref{sec:4239}); by (iv) this is consistent with $\|k-P_Kk\|_Q$ being small
for $K\ge10$. A quantitative rate in (iv) would follow from regularity of $k$, which we do not pursue here.
\end{remark}

\section{Certification}\label{sec:cert}

The optimisation is carried out in double precision. The resulting coefficients are converted to exact rationals $c_k\in\Q$, and $L$ is taken to be an exact decimal. For this fixed $f=\sum c_k b_k$ we evaluate $W_E(f)/\hat f(0)^2$ rigorously in ball arithmetic (Arb~\cite{Arb} via \texttt{python-flint}, $256$-bit precision):
\begin{enumerate}
\item On $[0,2L]$, $g=f*f$ is a polynomial with rational coefficients. We compute it exactly in $\Q[u]$ and obtain $\hat f(0)$ exactly.
\item The archimedean integral is split at $u=1/4$. On $[0,1/4]$ we integrate $(g(0)-g(u))/u$ against the Bernoulli expansion $u/(e^u-1)=\sum_n B_n u^n/n!$, truncated after $80$ terms. The tail is bounded with $|B_n|/n!\le 4(2\pi)^{-n}$. On $[1/4,2L]$ the integrand is analytic and is integrated rigorously by Arb's certified Gauss--Legendre integration. The constants $\gamma_{\mathrm E}$, $\pi$ and the logarithms are balls. The remaining part $\int_{2L}^\infty g(0)\,du/(e^u-1)=-g(0)\log(1-e^{-2L})$ is evaluated in closed form in Arb, as are $-\gamma_{\mathrm E}g(0)$ and $g(0)\log(N/4\pi^2)$.
\item The prime sum runs over all prime powers $n<e^{2L}$. The quantities $\log n$, $\sqrt n$ and $c_n$ are balls computed from the exact integers $a_p$. If the ball for $\log n$ contains $2L$, the window is rejected.
\end{enumerate}
The output is a ball containing $W_E(f)/\hat f(0)^2$. A statement $\ran(E)\le r$ is recorded only if the upper end of the ball is below the target: $r+1$, or $r+2$ when the root number is $(-1)^r$ (parity).

\subsection*{Large prime counts}
For the curves of Sections~\ref{sec:records} and~\ref{sec:board} the prime sum has up to about $3.0\cdot10^{10}$ terms, and two further tools are used.

\emph{Traces of Frobenius.} For the curves of Tables~\ref{tab:icarm} and~\ref{tab:historic} the $a_p$ with $p<10^9$ are computed with PARI/GP~\cite{PARI}. For \#4239, and for all $a_p$ with $p\ge1000$ in Section~\ref{sec:board} (board, closing runs and $2$-torsion curves), we use our own implementation of the baby-step giant-step method of Shanks and Mestre on the curve and its quadratic twist: the whole Hasse interval is searched, the parity of $a_p$ is read off from the $2$-torsion of $E(\mathbb F_p)$, and candidate sets from several points are intersected until a single value remains; the program never guesses and aborts otherwise. Every value is finally re-checked on fresh points of $E$ and of its twist before it is written. On \#4239 it reproduces all $50\,847\,534$ values of PARI for $p<10^9$ bit for bit, and on \#3762 and \#3900 the board certificates computed from these $a_p$ coincide digit for digit with the PARI-based certificates behind Table~\ref{tab:icarm}.

\emph{Prime sum.} The sum in step~3 can also be enclosed by a second, independent implementation in $\mathtt{f64}$ interval arithmetic: every operation is rounded to nearest and widened outward by one unit in the last place, $\log p$ is computed from the exact reduction $p=m2^e$ and a bounded series for $\operatorname{artanh}$, $\log 2$ is an interval checked against Arb, and $g$ enters through its exact Chebyshev coefficients on $[0,2L]$, rounded outward. All other parts of $W_E(f)/\hat f(0)^2$ are still computed in Arb. On every curve on which both were run the two certificates agree within their radii and give the same verdict; the interval version is about $10$ times faster per core and its enclosures are wider.

Which certifier was used is recorded in the certificate files. Tables~\ref{tab:icarm}--\ref{tab:4239} (including \#4239 at $p<e^{24.82}$) and the $349$ board curves completed with $p\le10^9$ are certified in Arb alone (\texttt{highrank/cert\_stream.py}, \texttt{validation/cert\_all.py}, \texttt{scripts/cert\_rap.py}); the $46$ board curves carried to $p<10^{10}$, the closing runs and the $2$-torsion curves are certified with the interval prime sum (\texttt{scripts/cert\_fast.py}). The radius of an Arb certificate is below $10^{-60}$; the radii printed in this paper are those of Arb's decimal output, which include the rounding of the printed midpoint (at most $5\cdot10^{-9}$ for ten significant digits and values near $30$), and they are always rounded up. With the interval prime sum the printed radii reach $7\cdot10^{-7}$ at $5\cdot10^9$ primes. On every one of the $790$ board entries the distance from the midpoint to the target is at least $440$ times the radius (tightest case: fixed kernel on \#3871 at $p<10^{10}$, distance $1.45\cdot10^{-4}$, radius $3.29\cdot10^{-7}$), so the width of the enclosure decides no case.

\section{Benchmarks}\label{sec:bench}

For each curve we scan $L$ on a grid of step $0.05$ with $K=10$ basis functions. For both $B_{\mathrm{std}}$ and $B_{\mathrm{rad}}$ we record the first $L$ at which the certified bound drops below $r+1$, where $r$ is the recorded rank (criterion~A), and the first $L$ at which it drops below $r+2$ (criterion~B; since the root number fixes the parity of $\ran$, this also gives $\ran\le r$). Cost is measured as the number of primes $p<e^{2L}$ used. Because of the grid, prime counts at the threshold are quantised. Ties are therefore frequent and slightly understate the gain.

\section{Curves of record rank}\label{sec:records}

For curves of large rank the cutoff, and with it the number of primes, grows quickly: Klagsbrun, Sherman and Weigandt used $\Delta=4$ (primes up to $e^{8\pi}\approx 8\cdot10^{10}$, about $40$ hours) for the Elkies curve~\cite{KSW}, and the leaderboard record of rank $30$ was certified with $\Delta=4.25$ (primes up to $\approx4\cdot10^{11}$)~\cite{ICARM}. This is where a better test function matters most. The following comparison was suggested by Z.~Klagsbrun.

\subsection*{Setup}
All curves are taken from the ICARM Elliptic Curve Rank Leaderboard~\cite{ICARM} (snapshot of 12 October 2026, $4201$ curves) in their global minimal models; the rank $r$ is the leaderboard's certified lower bound. We recomputed every conductor from local data with PARI, at the leaderboard's (verified) list of bad primes, and checked that the root number equals $(-1)^r$ in every case, so that a certified bound $B<r+2$ gives $\ran\le r$ under GRH (and rank exactly $r$ under GRH and BSD). We computed $a_p$ for all $p<10^9$ ($50\,847\,534$ primes), scanned $L$ with step $0.01$ and $K=10$ (for $E_{28}$, $K=14$ and $K=20$ give the same values to $10^{-4}$), and certified every value in Tables~\ref{tab:icarm}--\ref{tab:4239} with Arb as in Section~\ref{sec:cert}; values quoted from the board of Section~\ref{sec:board} carry its certificates.

\section{The ICARM high-rank board}\label{sec:board}
Following a suggestion of Z.~Klagsbrun, we ran both kernels on every curve of the leaderboard with rank lower bound $24$--$31$ (snapshot of 17 October 2026, $395$ curves). The root number equals $(-1)^r$ for all of them, so the target is $r+2$: a certified bound below $r+2$ gives $\ran\le r$ under GRH, and $\ran=r$ under GRH and BSD. Every hit is certified as in Section~\ref{sec:cert}; when a kernel does not reach the target, its certified bound at the largest window is recorded instead. The full lists are in the repository (\texttt{highrank/board/}, \texttt{highrank/close/}). The first window below the target is located by bisection on the grid of step $0.01$, so the preceding grid point is above the target; since for fixed $K$ the bound need not decrease monotonically in $L$, an earlier crossing is not excluded. The board contains the historic record curves $E_{24}$ (\#10), $E_{27}$ (\#67) and $E_{28}$ (\#11) and the rank-$29$ curve of Elkies and Klagsbrun (\#12); on \#12 only the optimised kernel reaches the target with $p<10^{10}$.

\subsection*{\texorpdfstring{Comparison with $2$-Selmer bounds}{Comparison with 2-Selmer bounds}}
Jain's \texttt{rankbound}~\cite{Jain} bounds the $2$-Selmer rank, and hence the Mordell--Weil rank, through the class groups of the cubic field of the $2$-division polynomial, following~\cite{BDF,KSW}. It is conditional on GRH for finitely many Dedekind zeta functions, does not use BSD, and applies to curves without a rational point of order $2$. On the $395$ board curves (database at commit \texttt{f324d1b} of 22 October 2026) both methods determine the rank on $393$ curves. Ours alone does so on \#1902 (rank $26$), where the $2$-Selmer bound is $28$; \texttt{rankbound} alone does so on \#634, the curve left open above. In total \texttt{rankbound} determines $394$ of the $395$ curves and the explicit formula $394$; every curve of rank $24$--$31$ on the board has its rank determined by at least one of the two methods, each under its own hypotheses. The comparison is reproduced by \texttt{scripts/compare\_rankbound.py} from \texttt{highrank/board/summary.csv}, \texttt{highrank/close/summary.csv} and the pinned \texttt{rankbound} database.

\subsection*{\texorpdfstring{Curves with rational $2$-torsion}{Curves with rational 2-torsion}}\label{sec:torsion}
Jain's class-group method does not apply to curves with a rational point of order $2$, whose $2$-division polynomial is reducible; for these curves the Selmer groups are computed instead by descent via a $2$-isogeny, as in \texttt{mwrank}~\cite{Cremona}, which gives unconditional upper bounds. The explicit formula does not depend on the torsion. On the leaderboard (as of 25 October 2026) $44$ curves of rank at least $20$ have a rational point of order $2$: $34$ of rank $20$, $8$ of rank $21$ and $2$ of rank $22$. They form $22$ pairs of $2$-isogenous curves (checked with PARI's \texttt{ellisomat}); isogenous curves have the same $L$-function, so each pair shares one certificate ($22$ distinct $L$-functions). With primes below $10^{8}$ the optimised kernel certifies $\ran\le r$ under GRH on all $44$, using at most $731\,001$ primes. The fixed kernel also succeeds on all of them and needs $1.81$--$2.90$ times as many primes (median $2.35$). We have not compared with $2$-isogeny descent on these curves.

\section{From primes to time}
\label{sec:time}

Counting primes is the natural measure for this method, because the traces $a_p$ dominate the
cost on curves of large conductor. It is not, however, the same as wall-clock time, and the
difference matters.

In the board computation of Section~\ref{sec:board} the prime bound is raised in powers of ten and
the traces are recomputed from scratch at each stage. On the machine used (an Apple M-series laptop, all $14$ cores, $0.5$ TB disk) the median measured times to produce all $a_p$ below $P$ were $0.4$, $0.5$, $4.8$, $56.3$ and
$705.7$ seconds for $P=10^6,\dots,10^{10}$. The staged search therefore costs the sum of the stages up
to the one that succeeds, not the last stage alone: $767.7$ seconds for a curve carried to the cap,
against $705.7$ for its final stage, a factor of $1.09$. Aggregated over the board the factor is also
$1.09$; for a curve whose last stage is $10^7$ it is $1.8$, because the stage times are not
proportional to $P$. For the curves continued beyond $1.2\cdot10^{11}$ (Table~\ref{tab:close}) the traces are instead stored on disk and extended block by block, so each stage only adds the new primes; the stored traces then grow with $P$, and on the $0.5$ TB disk of this machine the run for \#634 had to stop after $p<5\cdot10^{11}$. For \#535 the whole closing run took $104\,618$ seconds (about $29$ hours), of which the traces took about $95\%$.

On the $46$ curves run to the cap the traces account for a median of $93.8\%$ of the measured wall-clock time (range $91.4$--$95.2\%$). The rest covers the $L$-scan for \emph{both} kernels and the certification at
every stage, and therefore bounds the extra cost of the optimised kernel on these curves. This is the case most favourable to the traces; for a curve that succeeds at
$10^6$ the certification dominates instead.

Assigning to each kernel the stage at which it first reaches the target, and costing that stage by
the five median times, gives over the $372$ curves on which both succeed a total trace cost of
$2.48$ hours for the optimised kernel against $5.54$ hours for the fixed one, a factor of $2.23$.
These hours are computed from five median timings, not measured curve by curve, and cost only the final stage. The factor is markedly
less than the $3.95$ median of the per-curve prime ratios, and the reason is the granularity of the
search: a curve that needs twice as many primes usually falls in the same decade, so the saving is
not realised. On the $203$ curves where the two kernels fall in different decades the picture is:

So even among these the gain in time ranges from a factor of $1.25$ to a factor of $147$, and on $79$ of

In summary, in the regime where this method is used at all, the saving in
primes translates into a saving in time of the same order, but only once the prime counts differ
by enough to cross a decade. A search on a finer grid of prime bounds would recover more of it.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item All bounds assume GRH for $L(E,s)$ and concern the analytic rank. Statements about the algebraic rank additionally use points, the root number and, for $\ran\le1$, the theorems of Gross--Zagier~\cite{GZ} and Kolyvagin~\cite{Kolyvagin}.
\item The basis size is fixed at $K=10$ and the cutoff grid at step $0.05$ ($0.01$ in Sections~\ref{sec:valid} and~\ref{sec:records}). Larger $K$ can only improve $B_{\mathrm{rad}}$, and by Theorem~\ref{thm:main} the bounds converge as $K\to\infty$; the theorem gives no rate, and the saturation at $K\approx10$ is an observation. A finer grid would turn some ties into gains. We have not optimised either choice.
\item Certification uses Arb, and for the prime sum of large instances an independent $\mathtt{f64}$ interval implementation (Section~\ref{sec:cert}); both are written by the author and cross-checked against each other, not formally verified.
\item The gain depends on the curve: on record-rank curves it ranges from a factor of about $2$ ($E_{20}$, $E_{21}$) to $6.4$--$6.5$ (ICARM, ranks $28$--$31$) for a bound below $r+2$; on the board the ratio of prime counts ranges from $1.87$ to $14.95$ over the $372$ curves on which both kernels succeed. \#3593 required $p<2.8\cdot10^{11}$ and \#535 $p<8\cdot10^{11}$; on \#634 neither kernel reaches the target with $p<5\cdot10^{11}$; that run was stopped for lack of free disk space (Section~\ref{sec:time}, Table~\ref{tab:close}).
\item Throughout, cost is measured as the number of primes, that is the number of traces $a_p$
required. Section~\ref{sec:time} relates this to wall-clock time; the ratio in time is smaller
than the ratio in primes. The evaluation cost of the kernels themselves is not separated out:
per prime the optimised kernel additionally evaluates Chebyshev moments, and per window it
assembles and factors the Gram matrix, whereas the fixed kernel only accumulates a sum. Both
are contained in the non-$a_p$ share bounded in Section~\ref{sec:time}.
\item Ranks in Sections~\ref{sec:records} and~\ref{sec:board} are certified lower bounds from the leaderboard. Our bounds concern the analytic rank; equality with the algebraic rank requires BSD. One board curve, \#634, remains open for our method (Table~\ref{tab:close}).
\end{itemize}
